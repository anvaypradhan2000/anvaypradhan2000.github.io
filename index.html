I study how flat woven sheets become three-dimensional structures — and how to design the flat pattern that produces a shape you want.

I work with Evgueni T. Filipov and Talia Y. Moore at Michigan, on architected materials built from strips rather than solids: weaves that carry load through contact and friction, store energy in bending, and fold themselves out of the plane when a few of their strips are told to contract.

Research vision

My work starts with a physical system, builds mathematics that explains it, pushes that mathematics past the limits it was written for, and then carries the result back into something you can hold. Woven architected materials are where all three steps meet: the geometry is simple enough to write down, rich enough to be hard, and cheap enough to cut and test the same afternoon.

A woven sheet is an unusual kind of material. It has no continuum to speak of — just strips, contacts, and friction — yet it carries load and folds itself into shapes that were never drawn. I want a design language for that: a notation for the pattern, a model fast enough to search over, and a route from a target shape back to the flat sheet that makes it.
